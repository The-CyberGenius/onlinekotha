The Art of Closure: Why We Re-read Old Texts (And How to Finally Find Peace)

It’s 1:30 AM. You open WhatsApp, type a keyword from three years ago, and scroll through memories of a relationship that no longer exists. Why does the human brain do this? And how can understanding our digital footprints help us move on?

5 min read·October 2026

Almost everyone has done it. After a painful breakup, conventional advice urges us to "delete everything immediately and block them". Yet studies in digital grief show that a sudden deletion often triggers panic or regret.

When an intimate connection dissolves, our minds crave narrative coherence: Where did the warmth fade? Did I miss obvious signs? Was the love ever real? Your chat archive provides the objective reality that memory often distorts.

The Pitfall of Doomscrolling WhatsApp

Re-reading texts directly on WhatsApp is often dysregulating because it exists in an active messaging container—you see the empty typing bar, the last seen timestamp, and the phantom expectation of a reply.
